    Six rules for a good quiz

    1. Quiz twice a week, five minutes each. One round of 10 cards is about five minutes. Short and regular beats one long session.
    2. You do not need to know the math. Each card tells you what to say and shows the answer in symbols and in words. Match what you hear to what you see.
    3. No partial credit for almost. A wrong sign, a missing square root, or a long pause to work it out all count as Missed. The goal is instant recall.
    4. After a miss, say the answer aloud and move on. Do not stop to teach it. The card will come back on its own in a later round.
    5. Run the Friday check on paper. Blank page, timer, no notes. Write the result on the Friday checks tab so it sits next to the other weeks.
    6. Bring the miss list to tutoring. The Report tab shows the cards missed most. That list tells the tutor where to spend the session.

    How to read the symbols aloud

    π is "pi". θ is "theta". A small 2 above a function, as in sin², is "sine squared". The ± sign is "plus or minus". Each card writes the words out for you, so you only need this if your student asks.
